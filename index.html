What I noticed

Color Café is already a small production business, not only a café.

I went through the whole public side: every one of the 165 pages, all four studios, events, home workshops, the kit shop and the blog. What stood out is that the most complex part of the business, the journey of each physical piece, happens after the guest leaves and is invisible to them.

0studios, from Tel Aviv to Kiryat Ono and Hod Hasharon
0home-kit products in the online shop
0event types with their own pages
0hand-offs between booking and pickup for every piece

The dark steps happen behind the scenes. Today the guest sees none of them, so they call and ask.

When is it ready?

“in about a month”“about two weeks”“4–6 weeks”

Three different promises across the site. A real status replaces the promise.

Will you tell me?

“we'll send an SMS”“we don't send notifications”

Some pages promise a message, some older blog posts say there isn't one.

Where exactly?

Naomi Shemer 8Naomi Shemer 5two phone numbers

The same studio details are typed by hand on many pages, so they drift apart.

30 days

After a piece is ready it waits one month on the shelf, then it's donated. Every forgotten piece is a guest who paid, created something and never got the joy back. A gentle reminder with a map link, a photo of the finished piece, and a “deliver it to me” button turns that into a happy ending, and sometimes into extra revenue.

Everything else we could build

One system, many small joys.

The tracker is the spine. Everything below grows out of the same data: guests, visits, pieces, stock and events. Each idea is a separate step, and most of them are interactive here.

How it fits

Keep the website. Add a brain behind it.

The current site has years of search ranking behind it: 16 event pages and 43 blog posts. Rebuilding it from scratch risks all of that for little gain. The new layer plugs into what already works.

1
Wix stays the shop windowPages, the kit shop and forms stay where they are. Wix can send and receive data, so orders and requests flow into the new system automatically.
2
One source of truthAddresses, hours, prices and firing times live in one place and appear everywhere from there. No more “5 or 8”.
3
Works on any phoneA web app the team opens on a phone or tablet. Nothing to install from a store.
Why not an off-the-shelf tool? Studio trackers exist abroad (Kilnfox, ClayTrack), but they're English-only, email-first and built for a single studio. Color Café needs Hebrew, WhatsApp, Ontopo bookings, four branches with a shared kiln flow and transfers between them.

Roadmap

Step by step, starting where it hurts most.

Every step works on its own and pays back on its own. The order is my guess from the outside, and the right order comes from talking to you and the team.

1Quick wins

One source for addresses, hours and firing times. A clearer booking path and kit checkout.

start here2Pottery Tracker

QR per piece, kiln batches, shelves, pickup, WhatsApp “it's ready”.

3Inventory

Stock per studio and warehouse, transfers, low-stock alerts, glazes.

4Events CRM

From request to quote, deposit, event, firing and delivery.

5My Color Café

Guest page: bookings, creations, gift card, book again.

6Analytics

Studios, pieces, kiln load, events, all in one view.

7Redesign

A fresh, fast front end, built on top of the working system.

Pilot in one studioStart at one location with a label printer and the team's phones. Keep the current process running in parallel until the team trusts it.
A few weeks, not monthsA first working tracker is a few weeks of focused work, plus time to set up the business WhatsApp account.
Measured, not guessedOne week of baseline first: how long a pickup takes, how many “is it ready?” calls come in, how many pieces go unclaimed. Then we compare.

Before building anything

What I'd love to learn from you.

Which part of the day costs the team the most time or nerves today?
How many pieces a month go through the kilns, and are they fired centrally or in each studio?
How does a pickup work right now, and how often can a piece not be found?
Which register and tools do you use today: sheets, WhatsApp groups, Ontopo reports?
How many pieces end up unclaimed after 30 days?
What do guests ask most often by phone and WhatsApp?

Guests come back happier than they arrived. So should their pottery.

Let's spend half an hour in one of the studios, walk through a real day, and pick the first step together.
