Why start here

At our meeting on 30 September we agreed on the two biggest pains, and these steps take them on one by one:

  1. WhatsApp and phone. About 200 messages come in, and two team members answer non-stop. Most questions repeat: hours, directions, booking, home kits, "is my piece ready?". The current menu bot makes guests tap through 8 options, and most give up and wait for a person.
  2. Pieces and stock. A piece waits 4 to 6 weeks between painting and pickup, and nobody can see where it is. Big bisque shipments arrive in boxes with no inventory control. Management has no single view of what is where.

I checked your three nearby competitors (Clayla, Clay House, Clay Play). None of them tracks pieces or tells guests where their piece is. Clay Play's FAQ even has "what if I didn't get the pickup message?". Getting this right makes Color Café the studio that always knows where your piece is.

How the WhatsApp agent works

The guest just writes, the way they would write to a person. No menus. The agent answers from your own rules and prices, in the guest's language, and stays strictly on studio topics.

When a person takes over. Always: complaints, refunds and payment disputes, changes that can't be made by link, a photo of a damaged piece, large corporate events, a guest asking for a person, or two unclear messages in a row. The agent tells the guest honestly: "I'm passing you to the Ben Yehuda team, they'll reply by 14:00." The chat goes to the branch it is about, with a short summary. After that the agent stays quiet until the branch closes the chat or hands it back.

See it in action: the agent prototype, five real guest questions, today vs. with the agent.

Outside working hours and on Shabbat the agent collects the request and promises a reply at the next opening hour.

Safe rollout. For the first two weeks of step 1 the agent only drafts replies and staff press send. Then it answers by itself, only on the safe topics you approve. Everything else stays with staff. One switch turns the agent off, and staff keep answering from the same inbox.

Piece tracking and labels

Paper can't go into the kiln, so the piece carries its own short code on the bottom. The rule for the whole process: at most one new action per stage, under 10 seconds.

  1. Drop-off. Staff write a short code, like Y483, on the bottom with an underglaze pencil. This replaces the name you write today. In the app: guest phone, code, and a tick "send me a WhatsApp when it's ready".
  2. Glaze and kiln. The piece goes as usual. No labels, no load sheets.
  3. Unloading. Staff type or pick the code in the app and tap "ready". One photo of the whole shelf, not piece by piece. The guest gets the message.
  4. Pickup. Find by phone or code, tap "picked up". A QR sticker on the pickup bag only if it proves useful.

The kiln test comes first, at the start of step 2. 20 real pieces through a full firing: 2 to 3 pencils and pens, dipped and sponged bottoms, wax, small pieces, pieces with a painted bottom. If the code does not survive on 100% of them, we switch to a photo grid of each kiln shelf. Only after that do we build the app around the method that works.

Bisque boxes get a label per box or per batch, never per piece. A piece gets its code only when a guest paints it. If you have one central kiln, we add an "in transit" step marked per batch.

Privacy and data

The work follows Israel's Privacy Protection Law, including Amendment 13 (in force since August 2025).

  • Color Café owns all guest data. We sign a data processing agreement before I receive any export.
  • Phone numbers and names are removed from the export before it is used to train or test the agent.
  • The inbox runs on a server registered to Color Café. Access is limited to your team and me.
  • A guest gets a "ready" message only after ticking the consent box at drop-off. Marketing messages need separate consent and are not part of this plan.
